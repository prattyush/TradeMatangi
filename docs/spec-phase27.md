> **Decisions (S6, S7).**
> - **Score:** 0 to 100 per day, non-linear (penalties grow with repeats; bonuses are larger at the extremes), computed once the day's trades are all labelled, stored per day with the points of each rule. Shown on the day card, as a trend in Stats > Behavior and as a breakdown in the day's Notes tab. Weights live in one config so they can be tuned on real days.
> - **Rule 1 (drawdown against the guardrail limit):** the open-position drawdown is rebuilt from cached candles, with the day's cycles on one time axis, because the system stores no mark-to-market. From now on each session saves its guardrail capital-loss limit at create. **Older sessions use a fixed 12%** (the owner's earlier setup) and are marked "estimated". A day whose candles are missing skips this rule and is marked "partial".
> - **Rules 2 and 3 ("same direction"):** the **same contract** (the same CE or PE strike), within 2 to 3 bars (6 to 9 minutes), with the same expected strategy where it is labelled.
> - **System habits:** rules 2, 3 and 5 become habits named `sys:repeat_entry`, `sys:add_to_loser` and `sys:big_loss_wipes_gains`, shown in the Habits scorecard, trends and compare next to your own habits (they cannot be archived). Rules 1 and 4 are day-level and show as score items.
> - **Real trading:** the guardrail snapshot is written at session create (additive). That is a real-trading path, so it stays on `dev` until the owner's real-session try.
>
> **Built (S6 backend, S7 screens; see the S7 note below).** `GET /api/analysis/day-score?date=` returns the score of a market date: `status` is `scored`, `pending_labels` (some closed trade has no label yet; `labelled` of `closed` is reported) or `no_trades`; a scored day has the score, the base, one item per rule with its points and a plain sentence, the limit used and where it came from, the drawdown reached, and the events behind rules 2, 3 and 5. `GET /api/analysis/day-scores?start=&end=&compute=` lists stored scores for the Stats trend (with `compute=true` it also scores up to 31 days that have none yet, newest first, and says how many are left). **The numbers (all in one config, `CONFIG` in `day_score_service.py`):** a plain day is 60. **Drawdown:** the day's total P&L (closed trades plus open positions marked at the worst price of each minute from the cached candles; a long lot at the low, a short lot at the high) touched 50 % of the limit: -8; 75 %: -14; 100 %: -25; 150 %: -35. **Repeat entry:** a losing trade on the same contract and direction (same expected strategy when both are labelled) entered within 3 bars of the previous one closing at a loss: -6, then -10, -16, -24 for each further one in the chain. **Adding to a loser:** an entry added while losing where it and the entry before it both lost: -6, -10, -16, -24. **Clean day:** the running closed P&L never below zero over 3 or more trades: +15 (6 or more: +20). **One loss wiping the wins:** a loss at least as big as the three or more wins just before it: -10; twice as big: -16; three times: -22. The score is clamped to 0 to 100. **The limit** is the one the session was created with (`guardrail_limit_snapshot` on the Sessions row, written once at create, kept on resume); an older session uses a fixed 12 % and the result says `estimated`. Two sessions on one date use the strictest limit, and the largest capital. A day with missing candles is `partial` (the closed trades still count, so it is a lower bound). The score is stored per date (`DayScores`) and recomputed when a fill, a label or the numbers change. **The guardrail snapshot is a real-trading path (session create): dev only until your replay, paper, then real try.** The Windows app needs a rebuild (new analysis routes).
>
> **Built (S7, the screens).** (1) **The day card:** a chip "Score 72" (green 75 and above, amber 50 to 74, red below 50; the hover says "limit estimated" or "partial candles" when that applies) next to the labelled-progress badge. The list shows the stored scores at once (one cheap read, nothing is computed for a closed card); opening a card reads the day's full score, and every tab change reads it again, so a label saved in Label Trades is in it when you look. No chip while the day has closed trades without a label. (2) **The Notes tab** starts with the **Day score** breakdown: the score, the starting 60, then one line per rule with its points and what was found (costliest first, bonus last), the lowest point of the day against the limit, and a note when the limit is estimated or candles were missing; for a day that cannot be scored it says how many trades still need a label. (3) **System habits:** the three calculated habits appear in the Habits ledger as `sys:repeat_entry`, `sys:add_to_loser` and `sys:big_loss_wipes_gains` ("Re-entered within 3 bars of a loss and lost again", "Added to a losing position and both entries lost", "One loss wiped out three or more winning trades"), with a new **System** type beside Entry and Exit, in the scorecard, deep dive, trends and compare, with the same Costly / Rewarding verdicts as your own habits. They are not marks: they are worked out per market date from all of that date's sessions, they cannot be archived or edited, and a filter (a symbol, a mode) narrows them like the rest. (4) **Stats > Behavior:** the day-path trend has a **Day score (0 to 100)** metric: it scores the days in the range when chosen (up to 31 new days per round, four rounds), draws the scored days and leaves gaps for the others. The phone shows the System habits in Habits; it has no day card, so no chip or breakdown yet. Website, webapp and Windows app (rebuild).

### Found on 2026-11-22: a day that says "5 of 10 labelled" (owner's real and paper days)
- **What was seen:** the Notes tab said the day could not be scored (labels missing) while Label Trades showed every trade labelled and the date row said Labelled; in paper, "5 of 10 labelled" with 5 trades in Label Trades.
- **Decision (owner, 2026-11-22): one score per session type.** The log of 2026-11-06 showed the cause: a Paper BSESEN session (5 trades, all labelled) and a Replay NIFTY session of the same date (5 trades, none labelled) were added up. A day is now scored per session type: Replay (`sim`), Stepwise, Paper and Real each on their own sessions of the date, never together. Stored per user, date and type (`DayScores` sort key `<date>#<type>`; rows from before, keyed by the plain date, are ignored and scored again per type). `GET /api/analysis/day-score` takes `mode` (required); `/day-scores` returns one row per date and type (`mode` keeps one); the card scores its own type, the Notes tab says which ("Day score · Paper"), the Stats trend follows the selected day path's type, the Summary shows one score chip per type the day was traded in, and the system habits `sys:*` are worked out per type too. The note and the TODOs stay per date.
- **Why it happened:** the score used to be for the whole market date, so it added up every session of that date (each Start is a session, a linked screen on another symbol is one, and Replay and Stepwise runs of the same date count too), while Label Trades and the Labelled badge show one card (date, symbol, mode). Also found and fixed: a label saved without a cycle id (every Replay or Paper label, and any label saved while the trade was open) did not reach its trade when a fill crossed flat (legacy view: one trip with the whole exit quantity; cycles: two). Analysis matched such a label by index and showed it green, the score did not. `performance_service` now falls back to the set of entry orders (unique match only).
- **What the screens say now:** a pending day names the sessions (mode, symbol, count) and the entry time, strike and side of the trades still without a label.
- **Diagnostics:** a pending day writes one `DAYSCORE-DIAG` line (JSON) to `data/logs/backend.log` per change of its data: every session row of the date and why one was left out, per scored session the closed trades, the round trips Label Trades shows, the label rows saved (with or without a cycle id), the raw fills, and each unlabelled trade with its index and whether a label row has that index. Nothing in it changes a score.
